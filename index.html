How to convert HEIC to JPG

  1. Drop your .heic files into the box above (or click to browse).
  2. Choose JPG, PNG or WebP as the output format.
  3. Set the quality and max width to trade file size against resolution.
  4. Press Convert, then download each file or use Download all.

Everything runs inside your browser via the Canvas API and the open-source heic2any library. Your images are never sent to a server.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert HEIC to JPG?

Upload or drop your .heic files into the converter above, choose JPG as the output format, adjust the quality slider, then press Convert and download the result. Everything happens in your browser.

Is this HEIC converter really free?

Yes — there is no account, no watermark, no file limit and no paid tier. The tool is hosted on GitHub Pages at no cost.

Are my HEIC files uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion happens entirely inside your browser using the Canvas API and the heic2any library. Your files are never transmitted. You can verify this yourself: open your browser's Network tab and you will see no upload request at all.

How much can I compress a HEIC file?

On typical iPhone photos, converting to JPG at quality 75–80 while limiting the maximum width reduces the file size by 60–90% with no visible quality loss.

Can I convert multiple HEIC files at once?

Yes. Drop up to 50 HEIC files at a time and download them individually or as a batch.

Why can I not open HEIC files on my Windows PC?

Windows does not natively support HEIC. Convert them to JPG once with this tool and they will open everywhere — including in email clients, Microsoft Word, Photoshop and older software.

Which devices create HEIC files?

All modern iPhones and iPads (iOS 11+), plus many Android cameras and apps such as Google Photos, WhatsApp and Snapchat. macOS can also export to HEIC.

Will the converted JPG keep my photo's quality?

At quality 80 the difference is invisible to the eye. HEIC itself is a lossy codec, so converting to JPG never adds visible artefacts — it only re-encodes what HEIC already stored.
